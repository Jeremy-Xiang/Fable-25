GLOAMING

A draggable dusk sky, built from real scattering physics

01 The idea

Most "sky" web graphics are a CSS gradient with a couple of stops. GLOAMING instead computes the sky color per-pixel from an approximation of how sunlight actually scatters through an atmosphere — the same Rayleigh/Mie single-scattering model used in physically based sky renderers — so dragging the sun doesn't crossfade between presets, it re-runs the integral and the whole dome repaints correctly: blue zenith, warm horizon, red gloaming band, all falling out of the math rather than being hand-picked.

02 The scattering model

  • Per pixel, a view ray is cast into a spherical atmosphere shell around an idealized planet (radius 6,360km, atmosphere top 6,420km — Earth-scale constants).
  • The ray is marched in 12 steps; at each sample, a second ray is marched 5 steps toward the sun to find how much light survives to reach that point (the "light ray" / transmittance term).
  • Rayleigh scattering (wavelength-dependent, β_R ≈ (5.5, 13.0, 22.4)×10⁻⁶) makes blue scatter more than red — the reason the zenith is blue and the horizon reddens as the light path lengthens.
  • Mie scattering (grey, forward-biased via a Henyey-Greenstein phase function with g=0.76) adds the hazy glow around the sun disk itself.
  • Both terms are summed with their phase functions and exponential extinction, then tone-mapped (Reinhard-style 1-exp(-x)) and gamma-corrected.

03 Type & color

Antic Didot + Fragment Mono · no fixed palette — every color on screen is a shader output driven by sun elevation

04 The screenshot pass

The first headless render showed a sky that looked plausible but the hero read as slightly empty — no sense of scale or place. Added a two-layer procedural ridge silhouette along the bottom of the viewport (a simple sine-based jagged path, tinted near-black) so the sky has something to sit behind, and moved the drag-hint text down so it no longer crowded the header on narrow viewports. Also caught that the on-screen HUD readouts could sit flush against the bottom credit line at small heights; added spacing so both stay legible.

05 The complexity upgrade

A gentle idle auto-drift: until the visitor first drags or presses an arrow key, the sun slowly sweeps through a dawn-to-dusk arc on its own (a couple of offset sine waves on azimuth and elevation), so the piece never sits static waiting to be discovered — and it stops immediately and permanently once you touch it, handing control over cleanly. Fully disabled under prefers-reduced-motion.

06 Reproduce it

  1. Fullscreen quad + orthographic camera, one ShaderMaterial — no 3D scene needed
  2. Map screen UV to a wide-FOV elevation/azimuth ray direction (a "sky dome" camera)
  3. Ray-sphere intersect against atmosphere + planet radii to bound the march
  4. Nest a short light-ray march inside the primary march for transmittance
  5. Sum Rayleigh + Mie contributions weighted by their phase functions, tone-map, gamma-correct
