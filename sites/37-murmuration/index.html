A thousand birds,
one decision.

No leader, no plan — just three rules repeated a thousand times a second: stay close, match your neighbors, don't collide. Move your cursor into the flock. It scatters like there's a hawk in it, because as far as the simulation is concerned, there is.

01 — The rules

Cohesion, alignment, separation.

Each of the birds on this page is a boid: a point with a velocity, blind to the whole flock and aware only of its nearest neighbors. It steers gently toward their average position, matches their average heading, and swerves to avoid crowding them. That's the entire brain. The shape you see emerging is nobody's decision.

02 — The predator

Fear travels through a flock like a wave.

Your cursor carries a radius of avoidance. Birds inside it peel away hard; the birds next to them, sensing the sudden change in their neighbors' heading, peel away a beat later. The ripple you see moving through the flock away from your pointer isn't animated — it's the same three rules, just reacting to a threat none of them can actually see.

03 — The sky

One generated frame, kept honest.

The dusk gradient and the distant tree line are a single Higgsfield still, chosen deliberately abstract so it reads as atmosphere rather than a competing flock. Every bird you actually watch move is canvas-drawn and live.
