Heightmap-parallax — scroll to move through it

Four ridgelines,
one photograph,
never the same twice.

Every band of sand on this screen is the same source image, resampled at a different scroll speed and a different vertical offset. Scroll down and the dunes separate — the horizon barely moves, the near ridge rushes past.

01 — FAR RIDGE

The horizon holds still
on purpose.

The back layer moves at a fraction of your scroll speed — the way a real horizon barely shifts no matter how far you walk. Everything else is measured against it.

02 — MID RIDGE

Wind writes a new
edge every few pixels.

Each ridgeline is a value-noise silhouette, not a straight cut — the same trick that makes a real dune crest look drawn by wind rather than a ruler.

03 — NEAR RIDGE

The closer the sand,
the faster it runs.

Foreground bands travel several times faster than the horizon for the same scroll distance — ordinary parallax, tuned by hand until it reads as depth instead of a slideshow.
