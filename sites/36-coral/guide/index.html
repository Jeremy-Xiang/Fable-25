ABYSSA

The reef that makes its own light

01 The idea

ABYSSA is Wing II's first real-3D showcase and its first generated-asset piece: a Higgsfield still of a bioluminescent reef sits behind a genuine Three.js scene, not in front of it as a static hero. The image gives the color and richness a pure shader can't fake quickly; the live particle field and procedural coral give it the motion and depth a photo never can. Scroll dollies the camera forward through the reef — a depth readout in the corner tracks simulated meters — and the pointer nudges the whole scene like you're actually holding still against a current.

02 The craft

  • Three.js loaded via import map from unpkg, same pattern as the Wing I 3D pieces — no bundler, one module script
  • The hero image is a textured plane fixed far behind the camera (z −42), rescaled on resize to always fill the frustum — a real backdrop, not a CSS background
  • ~1,100 plankton as a single THREE.Points buffer, each with independent drift velocity and a per-particle cyan→magenta color sampled at init, additive blending for glow
  • Five procedural coral pieces built from TorusKnotGeometry and LatheGeometry with emissive materials — no modeled assets, just math and light
  • Depth is sold by three layers moving at three different parallax rates: backdrop barely, plankton some, coral (nearest) most
  • Feature-detects WebGL before touching the canvas; if it's unavailable the script never runs and a plain image fallback with a visible notice takes over instead

What the three passes caught: the build pass found the depth cue was unconvincing until camera dolly was tied to scroll instead of just autonomous drift — a static camera made the "parallax" claim in the copy false. The code-review pass flagged the plankton loop reading every particle every frame with no pooling; kept it since 1,100 points is cheap, but capped delta-time to avoid a tab-away spiral. The live-browser pass caught the background plane not covering ultra-wide viewports on resize (frustum math used the wrong aspect at first), and found prefers-reduced-motion wasn't actually freezing the plankton drift — only the camera; fixed both.

03 Type & color

Bricolage Grotesque + Martian Mono · abyssal near-black #050b14, bioluminescent cyan #3ff0d8, reef magenta #e64fe0

04 Reproduce it

  1. Generate one atmospheric hero image (nano_banana_pro), optimize with sips -Z 1920 -s formatOptions 78 to keep it well under 1MB
  2. Build a Three.js scene with an import-map module script; feature-detect WebGL first and provide an image-only fallback path
  3. Put the generated image on a plane far behind the camera, sized to the view frustum at that distance
  4. Layer a particle system and a few procedural meshes in front, each responding to pointer and scroll at a different rate for genuine parallax depth
  5. Wire prefers-reduced-motion to freeze every per-frame mutation, not just the obvious one — check each loop individually
