Bioluminescent reef · real-time 3D

The reef writes its
own light, not ours.

A generated deep-sea backdrop sits behind a live particle field of drifting plankton and a handful of procedural coral — a real Three.js scene, not a video. Move your cursor. Scroll down. The camera actually swims.

↓ descend
01 — Depth

Nothing down here needs the sun.

Past a few hundred meters, sunlight gives out entirely. What's left is a handful of species that learned to produce their own — cold, chemical, unmistakably alive. ABYSSA is a small study of that light: a still frame from Higgsfield layered with a live particle system so the glow actually moves.

02 — Plankton

Every point of light is doing its own thing.

Roughly a thousand individually-driven particles drift on independent velocities and gentle noise, tinted across the cyan-to-magenta range sampled from the backdrop. None of them share a path — that's what keeps the field from reading as a loop.

03 — Coral

Procedural growth, not modeled growth.

The foreground shapes are torus-knots and lathes built from math, not sculpted — each one seeded with a different twist and radius so no two read the same, then lit with emissive cyan and magenta cores like the real thing.

04 — Depth cue

Parallax is the whole trick.

The backdrop plane barely moves. The plankton move more. The coral, being closest, moves most of all. Three depth layers responding at three different rates is what convinces your eye there's actually distance between them — genuine 3D, not a painted illusion.
