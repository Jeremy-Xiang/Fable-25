A supercell photograph, generated once — everything that moves across it is math. Every bolt below is built live from recursive branching and mid-point displacement, no two strikes ever the same shape. Click anywhere. The rumble always arrives late.

click / tap / press enter to strike · auto-strikes every 6–14s STRIKES: 0
01 — The branch

Nature doesn't draw straight lines.

Each bolt starts as one straight segment from cloud to ground. The generator splits it at the midpoint, nudges that point sideways by a random amount, and repeats on both halves — a dozen or so times — until a jagged, unrepeatable path falls out the other end. It's the same mid-point displacement trick used for procedural coastlines and mountain ranges, aimed straight down instead.

02 — The fork

Some branches don't make it down.

At a few points along the main channel, the generator rolls the dice on spawning a side branch — shorter, fainter, dying out a few subdivisions later. Real lightning does the same thing for the same reason: the path of least resistance forks constantly, and most forks lose.

03 — The delay

Light first. Then the rest.

The strike flashes the screen white immediately. A beat later — timed, not audio, this page makes no sound — a faint pulse moves through the frame like a shockwave arriving after the light already has. It's a visual stand-in for the gap between seeing a strike and feeling the thunder.
