Site 94 · Wave 6 · how it was made

PRESSWORK

A live CMYK halftone press: four rotated dot screens print the poster, and your cursor shoves the plates out of register

01 The idea

Print production as an interaction: the poster is separated into real C/M/Y/K halftone screens at authentic angles (15°/75°/0°/45°), dot radius ∝ ink density, composited with multiply. Moving your cursor displaces each plate along a different vector — the misprint fringing every print nerd recognizes.

02 How it works

  • A hidden canvas holds the source art; cmykAt() converts RGB per sampled point.
  • Each plate walks its own rotated grid (the screen angle), drawing dots sized pitch·0.62·√ink — the sqrt is what makes midtones read correctly.
  • K stays registered while C/M/Y take scaled cursor offsets plus a slow breathing wobble; multiply blending does the ink math.
  • Three procedural posters rotate via NEXT POSTER; pitch and wobble are live sliders.

03 Type & color

  • Anton for poster type (halftones beautifully), Courier Prime for the shop floor.
  • Warm print paper; the CMYK primaries are the palette.

04 The screenshot pass

  • Verified rosette formation at rest (correct screen angles produce the classic pattern), fringe direction under cursor, and legible type at 9px pitch; checked slider extremes for dot overlap collapse.

05 Lift this into your site

  • plate() + cmykAt() convert any canvas into halftone separations — photos included.
  • Screen angles matter: keep K at 45° and ~30° separation or you get moiré instead of rosettes.
  • The whole effect is O(dots); at 6px pitch full-screen, render to an offscreen and reuse.
