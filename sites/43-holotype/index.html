01 — the shader

Five colors, chasing an angle.

The rainbow sheen is a single conic-gradient — magenta, gold, mint, cyan, violet — swept from an angle that tracks the cursor, blended onto the image with mix-blend-mode: color-dodge. Color-dodge blows the gradient out over the image's bright pixels and lets its dark pixels stay dark, which is exactly how a real foil card catches light unevenly across its printed art instead of washing it out uniformly.

02 — the tilt

Card space, not screen space.

The card sits inside a perspective container and rotates on both axes based on pointer offset from its own center — not the window's. That distinction matters: a card should tilt around itself, the way you'd turn one in your hand, not swing around some fixed point on the page. A soft radial glare layer and a diagonal foil-line layer move with the same pointer coordinates, one step behind the base rotation, so all three layers read as one physical object instead of three overlapping effects.

03 — try it

Tilt it yourself.

Move your cursor across the card above to rotate it and sweep the holo shader. On a phone, tap "Enable gyroscope tilt" and tilt the device instead — iOS requires that explicit tap before it will share motion data. No pointer or gyroscope? Focus the card and use the arrow keys; each press nudges the tilt by a fixed amount so the effect stays reachable by keyboard alone.
