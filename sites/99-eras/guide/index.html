THE LONG SCROLL

Thirty years of web design on one page — 1996, 2003, 2010, 2016, 2026 — each section period-correct

01 The idea

A scrollable museum where the exhibit is the medium itself: GeoCities blue with a blink tag and a hit counter, a 2003 glossy gradient box with badge wall, 2010 skeuomorphic leather-and-stitching, a 2016 flat purple startup with a pill CTA, and a 2026 mesh-gradient shader section. The fixed year readout flips as you cross each border.

02 How it works

  • Each era is scoped CSS cosplay: period fonts, period shadows, period sins (the 1996 marquee is a real animation; the 2010 icon's gloss is the canonical two-stop overlay).
  • An IntersectionObserver drives the giant mix-blend year numeral and the dot rail.
  • 2026 is a live canvas of four drifting radial-gradient blobs — the mesh-gradient look without WebGL.
  • Details carry the comedy: '0 drop shadows' stat card, 'best viewed at 800×600', visitors: 000042.

03 Type & color

  • Times → Verdana → Lobster → Inter → Space Grotesk: the fonts ARE the timeline.
  • Each era keeps its own palette; the shared year HUD in difference-blend survives all five.

04 The screenshot pass

  • Verified each era lands its references (ridge borders, gradient bar text-shadow, gloss overlay, flat purple) and the year/dots track section boundaries at the 55% threshold.

05 Lift this into your site

  • The scoped-era pattern is great for 'evolution' storytelling — changelogs, brand history, portfolio retrospectives.
  • Difference-blend fixed HUDs survive any background — steal that for multi-theme pages.
  • Period accuracy is a research task: one wrong border-radius and 2003 becomes 2013.
