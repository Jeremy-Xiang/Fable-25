Site 44 of 50 · how it was made

MAGNETO

The field you can't see, drawn live

01 The idea

MAGNETO is a Wing II piece: a Higgsfield-generated hero photograph — iron filings tracing a field around a bar magnet, macro, dramatic side light — sits dimmed behind a live canvas simulation of the same phenomenon the photo only freezes. Two poles sit on the canvas; drag either one and every field line on screen recomputes from scratch, thirty-odd times a second. The photo gives the piece texture and grain a shader can't fake; the canvas gives it motion a photo never could.

02 The craft

  • Generated hero (assets/hero.jpg) rendered at low opacity with a radial vignette, so it reads as a lit backdrop rather than competing with the live diagram
  • Field math: for every pole, an inverse-square contribution sign / (dx²+dy²+softening) summed at each sample point — the softening term keeps lines from blowing up right on top of a pole
  • Each field line is traced by stepping along the normalized local field direction for a fixed number of steps, from twenty seed points ringing every positive pole, and cut short if it wanders into a negative pole's capture radius
  • A cyan-to-magenta gradient runs along each traced line, plus a small direction arrow at its midpoint, so polarity and flow direction are legible at a glance
  • Ambient particles drift along the same field for a sense of live flow, independent of the field-line redraw, toggleable from the console
  • Poles are draggable with the pointer and, once tabbed to, movable with arrow keys — N adds a pole (up to five), R resets, Space/Enter cycles the active pole — the canvas carries a full ARIA label describing the controls
  • Under prefers-reduced-motion, particle drift stops and the loop renders a single static frame instead of a continuous requestAnimationFrame cycle

03 Type & color

Space Grotesk (display) + IBM Plex Mono (labels/console) · near-black studio bg, electric cyan #00f0ff, magenta #ff2ec4

04 Reproduce it

  1. Seed two or more poles as mutable {x, y, sign} objects, not functions — you need to drag them
  2. Write fieldAt(x,y) once, summing every pole's contribution; reuse it for both line tracing and particle drift
  3. Trace lines by stepping along the normalized field vector; break on out-of-bounds or on nearing a sink pole
  4. Gate any ambient particle motion behind prefers-reduced-motion, and skip the requestAnimationFrame loop entirely in favor of one render call when it's set

05 Wing II, asset pipeline

This is Wing II (sites 26–50), which trades Wing I's zero-binary-asset discipline for the generated-imagery pipeline: the macro iron-filings photograph behind the canvas is a Higgsfield nano_banana_pro still, used as a dimmed reference backdrop rather than the whole show — the signature technique is still the hand-written live simulation in front of it.
