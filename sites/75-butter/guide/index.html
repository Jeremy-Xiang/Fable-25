BUTTER

The lenis effect, hand-rolled — a lerped virtual scroll with parallax, in about sixty lines

01 The idea

Every "tools you need" reel names lenis, and every premium site seems to scroll like it's underwater in the best way. This demo strips the technique to its load-bearing core so you understand what you're importing: the page doesn't scroll — a number does, and the page eases after it. A live dock lets you flip between smooth and native mid-scroll and drag the lerp factor around until you feel where taste lives (spoiler: 0.08–0.12).

02 How the engine works

  • The document still scrolls natively. An empty spacer div is sized to the content height, so the scrollbar, keyboard paging, Space, Home/End, and accessibility tools all interact with a completely normal document. This is the trick that separates the lenis approach from the old scroll-hijacking sins — nothing intercepts the wheel.
  • The content is position:fixed and translated by the smoothed value: every frame, cur += (scrollY − cur) × lerp, then one translate3d. When |target − cur| < 0.1 the loop snaps and goes to sleep — zero idle cost.
  • Parallax is one multiplication. Elements carry data-speed; the same loop applies translate3d(0, cur × speed, 0). Negative drifts against the scroll, positive with it. No observers, no per-element listeners.
  • The progress bar reads scrollY / (scrollHeight − innerHeight) — it tracks the real scroll, so it stays honest even mid-glide.
  • Touch and reduced-motion get native scroll. Coarse pointers keep their platform inertia (fighting it feels broken), and prefers-reduced-motion disables the whole engine. Smoothing is a garnish, never a gate.

03 Type & color

  • Gabarito 900 for fat, friendly headlines; Atkinson Hyperlegible for body — a face literally designed for readability, on a page about not breaking reading.
  • Butter palette: warm paper #fdf8ef, toast ink #241d15, melted-butter orange #e0742c — the one accent doing progress bar, kickers, orbs, and dock states.

04 The screenshot pass

  • Anchor links were broken in smooth mode — and the page's own a11y card claimed they worked. With the content inside a fixed wrapper, a native #how jump computes against the wrong geometry and lands nowhere. Caught by a headless click-test (scrollY stayed 0), fixed with a delegated click handler that resolves the target's position inside the virtual page (target.top − view.top) and calls scrollTo — the lerp then glides there. Re-test: scrollY 758. This is the classic virtual-scroll footgun, now documented instead of hidden.
  • Engine verified numerically: mid-flight transform −908px against a 1200px target (lag = lerp working), settled at exactly −1200px, parallax orb at −216px = 1200 × −0.18, native toggle clears every transform and zeroes the spacer.
  • Full-page screenshots render correctly in smooth mode because the spacer keeps document height honest — a nice side-effect of not hijacking anything.

05 Lift this into your site

  • What to copy: the ≈60-line script block (everything above the dock wiring) plus two CSS rules: body.smooth #view{position:fixed; inset:0 0 auto 0} and #view{will-change:transform}. DOM contract: a #spacer before a #view wrapper around all page content.
  • Anchor links: bring the delegated click handler with you — it's mandatory, not optional (see 04).
  • Fixed-position children: anything position:fixed must live outside #view (here: header, dock, progress bar) or it will translate with the page.
  • Tunables: CFG.lerp (0.08–0.12 is the taste zone; lower = heavier), per-element data-speed (keep |speed| under 0.25 or content escapes its section).
  • When to just use lenis: the moment you need nested scrollable areas (modals, code blocks, horizontal sections), touch smoothing, infinite scroll, or scroll-triggered timeline syncing. This demo is the concept for learning and for pages simple enough to own their scroll; lenis is ~3KB of edge cases you'd otherwise rediscover one bug report at a time. Both can coexist with everything else here — the parallax pattern works identically on lenis's scroll value.

06 Reproduce it

  1. Wrap content in #view, add an empty #spacer sibling, size it to view.scrollHeight.
  2. Fix the view, lerp a number toward scrollY in rAF, apply one transform.
  3. Sleep the loop when converged; wake it from the scroll listener.
  4. Add data-speed parallax in the same loop.
  5. Delegate anchor clicks to virtual coordinates. Gate everything on pointer type and reduced-motion.
